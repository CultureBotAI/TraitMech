phage defense system

traitmech:000209 · CLASS · PROPOSED

A genomics trait describing possession of one or more bacterial or archaeal immune systems that inhibit bacteriophage infection.

Trait evidence (5)

  • DOI:10.1038/s41579-019-0278-2
    Viruses and their hosts are engaged in a constant arms race leading to the evolution of antiviral defence mechanisms

    Bernheim and Sorek define the bacterial bacteriophage-defense context and review the pan-genomic diversity and cost of antiviral defense systems.

  • DOI:10.1038/s41579-019-0278-2
    individual microorganisms often encode multiple distinct defence systems

    The pan-immune-system model supports treating possession of one or more defense systems as a strain-level genomics trait rather than a single protein, locus, or pathway.

  • DOI:10.1038/s41586-019-1894-8
    Bacteria have evolved numerous immune mechanisms, both innate and adaptive

    Hampton et al. review innate and adaptive anti-phage systems and the phage counter-defense mechanisms that evade them.

  • DOI:10.1038/s41586-019-1894-8
    Here we review the spectrum of anti-phage systems and highlight their evasion by bacteriophages

    This supports the umbrella scope spanning diverse CRISPR-Cas, restriction-modification, BREX, DISARM, abortive-infection, and related anti-phage defenses.

  • DOI:10.1126/science.aar4120
    Candidate immune systems were then experimentally validated for their activities

    The Science article summary for Doron et al. supports genomic discovery and experimental validation of antiphage defense systems from microbial pangenomes.

Antiphage defense systems resist viral challenge

Evidence-backed ontology sketch linking phage pressure to the umbrella phage-defense-system trait and its reviewed CRISPR-Cas and restriction-modification children.

NONMECHANISTIC · This parent trait groups independently reviewed genome-level anti-phage defense systems without claiming that CRISPR-Cas, restriction-modification, BREX, DISARM, abortive infection, or other defense families share one universal molecular mechanism.
