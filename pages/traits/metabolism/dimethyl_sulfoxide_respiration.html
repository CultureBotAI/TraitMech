dimethyl sulfoxide respiration

traitmech:000201 · CLASS · PROPOSED

An anaerobic respiration in which an organism uses dimethyl sulfoxide as the terminal electron acceptor for energy conservation.

Trait evidence (5)

  • DOI:10.1073/pnas.0505959103
    one (SO1427-SO1432) is required for anaerobic respiration of DMSO

    Gralnick et al. identify the SO1427-SO1432 gene cluster required for Shewanella oneidensis MR-1 anaerobic respiration of dimethyl sulfoxide.

  • DOI:10.1073/pnas.0505959103
    DMSO respiration is an extracellular respiratory process

    Gralnick et al. support DMSO respiration as a bona fide respiratory process rather than an isolated reductase assay.

  • DOI:10.1111/1462-2920.15971
    M. thermoacetica can also use dimethyl sulfoxide as terminal electron acceptor

    Rosenbaum et al. support organism-level use of dimethyl sulfoxide as an anaerobic terminal electron acceptor.

  • DOI:10.1111/1462-2920.15971
    Membranes showed a DMSO reductase activity

    Rosenbaum et al. biochemically support an induced DMSO reductase activity in Moorella thermoacetica membranes.

  • DOI:10.1073/pnas.0505959103
    Here, we identify two gene clusters in Shewanella oneidensis strain MR-1 that each contain homologs of genes required for metal reduction and genes that are predicted to encode dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) reductase subunits.

    Gralnick et al. identify the Shewanella oneidensis MR-1 gene clusters encoding the extracellular dimethyl sulfoxide reductase subunits.
