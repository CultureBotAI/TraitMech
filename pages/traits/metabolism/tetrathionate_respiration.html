tetrathionate respiration

traitmech:000200 · CLASS · PROPOSED

An anaerobic respiration in which an organism uses tetrathionate as the terminal electron acceptor and reduces it to thiosulfate for energy conservation.

Trait evidence (5)

  • DOI:10.1046/j.1365-2958.1999.01345.x
    A range of bacteria are able to use tetrathionate as a terminal respiratory electron acceptor

    Hensel et al. support tetrathionate as an organism-level terminal electron acceptor for anaerobic respiration.

  • DOI:10.1046/j.1365-2958.1999.01345.x
    ttrA, ttrB and ttrC are the tetrathionate reductase structural genes

    Hensel et al. identify the TtrA/TtrB/TtrC structural genes for Salmonella tetrathionate reductase.

  • DOI:10.1128/JB.183.8.2463-2475.2001
    Salmonella reduces tetrathionate to thiosulfate

    Price-Carter et al. support thiosulfate as the reduction product of Salmonella tetrathionate respiration.

  • DOI:10.1128/JB.183.8.2463-2475.2001
    the electron acceptor tetrathionate, which allows Salmonella to grow anaerobically on ethanolamine or 1,2-propanediol

    Price-Carter et al. support tetrathionate-dependent anaerobic growth in Salmonella enterica serovar Typhimurium LT2.

  • DOI:10.1046/j.1365-2958.1999.01345.x
    Sequence analysis suggests that TtrA contains a molybdopterin guanine dinucleotide cofactor and a [4Fe-4S] cluster, that TtrB binds four [4Fe-4S] clusters, and that TtrC is an integral membrane protein containing a quinol oxidation site.

    Hensel et al. characterize the subunit composition of Salmonella enterica serovar Typhimurium LT2 tetrathionate reductase (TtrA, TtrB, and TtrC).
