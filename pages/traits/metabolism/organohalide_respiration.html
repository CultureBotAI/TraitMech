organohalide respiration

traitmech:000204 · CLASS · PROPOSED

An anaerobic respiration in which an organism uses an organohalide as the terminal electron acceptor for energy conservation.

Trait evidence (3)

  • DOI:10.1098/rstb.2012.0322
    Organohalide respiration is an anaerobic bacterial respiratory process that uses halogenated hydrocarbons as terminal electron acceptors during electron transport-based energy conservation.

    Hug et al. define organohalide respiration as anaerobic electron-transport-based energy conservation with halogenated hydrocarbons as terminal electron acceptors.

  • DOI:10.1098/rstb.2012.0322
    Organohalide-respiring bacteria have been identified from multiple bacterial phyla

    Hug et al. support the record as a reusable bacterial respiration phenotype rather than a single-strain label.

  • DOI:10.1126/science.276.5318.1568
    Growth of strain 195 with H2 and tetrachloroethene as the electron donor and acceptor pair required extracts from mixed microbial cultures.

    Maymo-Gatell et al. support tetrachloroethene as a growth-coupled organohalide electron acceptor in Dehalococcoides mccartyi strain 195.
