ixotrophy

traitmech:000698 · CLASS · PROPOSED

A trophic-ecology phenotype in which filamentous bacteria capture microbial prey on their cell surface and kill the captured cells to obtain prey-derived nutrients.

Trait evidence (3)

  • DOI:10.1126/science.adp0614
    Single-cell analyses with stable isotope-labeled prey revealed that prey components are taken up by the attacker.

    Lien et al. 2024, PMID:39418385, scientific Abstract, not the separate article summary; directly matched source-qualified MED text. Introduction and contact/capture Results support the surface-trapping differentia. Nutrient-uptake Results, culture, killing, imaging and Raman Methods, Discussion and actual Figures 1, 5 and 6 read. Raman labeling supports uptake from killed prey; Figure 6 is a model, not independent flux data. On solid surfaces, gliding can establish contact without flagellar trapping; this record denotes a capability, not a mandatory sequence for every feeding event. Other figures, supplements and movies not fully assessed. Author-hosted published full text: https://stockerlab.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/183.Lien-et-al_Science_Kang.pdf. MED linked a commentary and preprint, not a correction/retraction; publisher status page was inaccessible, so that check remains limited.

  • DOI:10.1007/s002489900052
    can subsist on other microbes and can even catch motile bacteria by their flagella before killing and digesting them.

    Lewin 1997, PMID:9337418, directly retrieved publisher Abstract. The subject is Saprospira grandis; supports prey capture, killing and nutrition, not universal dependence on flagellated prey. MED metadata matched title and DOI but exposed no abstract; do not report an abstract-resolver verification for this quote. Full text, figures and strain provenance were unavailable. Publisher page and qualified MED metadata showed no linked correction/retraction at access, not proof none exists. Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s002489900052.

  • DOI:10.1099/mic.0.000158
    The ability of cells to aggregate or adhere is thought to be a significant initial step in ixotrophy.

    Furusawa et al. 2015, PMID:26306656, scientific Abstract directly read at the publisher and matched qualified MED metadata. CCB-QB1 adhesion/capture assays support an initial interaction, not by themselves nutrient transfer. Calcium and acidic polysaccharide interpretations are model-specific, not defining requirements. Full-text delivery failed; Methods, figures and supplement remain unread. Publisher page and MED record exposed no linked correction/retraction. Authors and CCB-QB1 material overlap with 2024, so these are not independent-group replications. Source: https://doi.org/10.1099/mic.0.000158.
