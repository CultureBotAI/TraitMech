periplasmic bacterial predation

traitmech:000699 · CLASS · PROPOSED

A predatory bacterial phenotype in which a bacterium enters the periplasm of a bacterial prey cell and grows and reproduces there using nutrients obtained by killing and consuming the prey.

Trait evidence (3)

  • DOI:10.1038/s42003-025-09010-x
    establishes itself in the prey periplasm, feeding on the prey’s cytoplasmic contents as a main nutrient source.

    Lai et al. 2025, PMID:41125861, PMC12546842, Introduction, second paragraph; the subject is B. bacteriovorus. Direct publisher HTML and Europe PMC full-text XML match. Figure 1a is a lifecycle schematic, not direct imaging; Figure 4a includes WT exit measurements. Other figures and supplements not fully assessed. Publisher and MED showed no linked notice; MED linked the earlier preprint, not a correction. Published source: https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-09010-x.

    Fenton et al. 2010, PMID:20935099, scientific Abstract retrieved from source-qualified MED metadata. Supports prey-derived nutrition during intrabacterial replication; do not generalize its single-prey wording to a fixed predator occupancy or universal prey dependence. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20935099/.

Predatory bacterium traitmech:000054 is broader; its intraperiplasmic_growth node denotes a lifecycle stage, not this whole feeding phenotype, and must not be equated to it. Epibiotic bacterial predation traitmech:000697 keeps the predator cell body external. Generic intracellular predation, endoparasitism and endosymbiosis are not exact synonyms of periplasmic predation. Attachment, killing without entry, or residence without nutritional growth are insufficient. Exact synonyms and xrefs remain unassigned pending authority and scope reconciliation. No organism-level disjointness, universal prey range, division pattern, progeny number or obligate dependence is asserted.
