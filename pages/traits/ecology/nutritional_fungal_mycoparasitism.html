nutritional fungal mycoparasitism

traitmech:000717 · CLASS · PROPOSED

An ecological phenotype in which a fungus obtains nutrients at the expense of another fungus, either from the living host or after killing it.

Trait evidence (3)

  • DOI:10.1139/b95-389
    The term mycoparasitism applies strictly to those relationships in which one living fungus acts as a nutrient source for another

    Jeffries 1995 review; scientific abstract directly retrieved from https://kar.kent.ac.uk/19621/. This strict sense contrasts with broader fungicolous usage in the same abstract. Selected main text and actual PDF pages 1 and 3 support detrimental nutrition with either a living host or killing before use. The record restricts actor and host to fungi, excluding historically grouped oomycetes. The review supplies terminology, not an independent experiment. Not every figure or table was inspected. Native abstract verifier: UNRESOLVED; the direct institutional excerpt was separately matched, not relabeled VERIFIED.

  • DOI:10.1002/rcm.6131
    In dual-culture the mycelia of T. atroviride which remained in direct contact with labeled A. mellea showed an increased δ(13)C value

    Pellegrini et al. 2012, PMID:22302490; directly retrieved scientific abstract, Results, using DOI and SRC:MED together. Title, DOI and PMID agree. After 29 days in contact, the reported carbon isotope value was 66.4 per mille versus -26.6 per mille with unlabeled host. This supports host-derived carbon assimilation, not by itself host killing, a penetration mechanism or exclusion of all alternative transfer routes. The journal full text was not retrieved; the restricted institutional deposit was not bypassed. The bacterial comparator is not an example of this fungal-actor trait. Native abstract verifier: VERIFIED.

  • DOI:10.1016/j.apsoil.2012.12.005
    Only T. harzianum maintained mycoparasitic activity in the soil microcosm

    Pellegrini et al. 2013; scientific abstract directly read at https://iris.unipv.it/handle/11571/604013?mode=complete. Native verifier: VERIFIED via AGR:IND500606635, not PubMed. The author-deposited Corneo thesis chapter identifies this DOI; its version is not presumed identical to the unread journal full text. Its experiment selects three microorganisms: the positive soil result concerns a sterile microcosm at 20 C after 29 days, followed by three-day culture-out before isotope measurement, not direct field flux or all 158 isolates. Dual-culture enrichment accompanies growth inhibition; the host viability endpoint comes from a separate broth assay. Washing and host-specific PCR address contamination without excluding every route of carbon transfer. Leakage versus direct parasitism remains partly interpretive. Shares all six authors with 2012, not independent-laboratory replication.

Nutrition at host expense is required; mere co-occurrence, fungicolous association, antagonism or utilization of already dead material does not establish it. Killing, penetration, obligacy and a single mechanism are not universal requirements. The unqualified mycoparasitism label is not an exact synonym. Local parasitism (traitmech:000043) inherits persistent physical association from symbiosis (traitmech:000040); that narrower local umbrella is not automatically applicable to destructive encounters included by Jeffries. METPO:1000059 phenotype is retained pending hierarchy review. Bacterial mycophagy (traitmech:000696) has a bacterial actor; microbial haustorium formation (traitmech:000663) is a structural phenotype, neither necessary nor sufficient for this nutrition. Saprotrophy (traitmech:000055) alone does not establish attack on a living host; no organism-level disjointness is implied. Pinned and current METPO broad lexical matches (0000261, 000262, 1000125, 1000197, 1000198, 1000227) are deprecated, with no replacement or consider annotation observed.
