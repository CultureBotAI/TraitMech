endophytic

traitmech:000442 · CLASS · PROPOSED

A host-associated trait in which a microbe resides within living internal plant tissues without causing apparent disease in the host.

Trait evidence (2)

  • DOI:10.1128/MMBR.00050-14
    All plants are inhabited internally by diverse microbial communities comprising bacterial, archaeal, fungal, and protistic taxa.

    Hardoim et al. frame endophytic lifestyles as internal microbial residence in plants.

  • DOI:10.3390/microorganisms5040077
    Endophytes are microbial symbionts residing within the plant for the majority of their life cycle without any detrimental impact to the host plant.

    Kandel et al. support the no-apparent-disease clause in the endophytic definition.

Internal plant colonization defines endophytism

Evidence-backed ecological sketch connecting living internal plant tissue to endophytic residence.

NONMECHANISTIC · This broad plant-host habitat trait spans bacteria, archaea, fungi, and protists and does not assert one universal molecular colonization mechanism.

Edge evidence

  • living internal plant tissue enables endophytic colonization RO:0002327

    Living plant interiors are the host tissue habitat where endophyte populations establish.

    • DOI:10.3390/microorganisms5040077 Endophytic colonization refers to the entry, growth and multiplication of endophyte populations within the host plant. Kandel et al. define endophytic colonization as establishment inside a plant host.
  • endophytic colonization confers endophytic METPO:2007700

    Endophytic colonization realizes the endophytic ecological trait.

    • DOI:10.3390/microorganisms5040077 Endophytes are microbial symbionts residing within the plant for the majority of their life cycle without any detrimental impact to the host plant. Kandel et al. define endophytes by internal plant residence without detrimental host impact.
