kin discrimination

traitmech:000716 · CLASS · PROPOSED

An ecological phenotype in which microbial cells preferentially associate with kin or exclude nonkin through differential interactions according to genetic similarity or relatedness.

Trait evidence (3)

  • DOI:10.1371/journal.pbio.0060287
    Our findings demonstrate that D. discoideum cells co-aggregate more with genetically similar than dissimilar individuals

    Ostrowski et al. 2008, PMID:19067487; scientific abstract, publisher PDF page 1, contiguous span visually checked. Mixtures with AX4-GFP and natural-isolate dye controls support partial sorting during aggregation, not complete clonal exclusion. The measured endpoint is variation in fluorescent spore proportions among fruiting bodies; genetic distance uses 12 microsatellite loci, not genome-wide identity. Main text and all four figures were inspected; Figure 1 is hypothetical. Supplementary Tables S1 and S2 were read by text extraction. Main Methods names QS45, absent from the retrieved S1 strain table; no strain alias is inferred. Kin-selected benefit is an interpretation, not a measured fitness endpoint here. Natural provenance of every laboratory derivative and a molecular recognition mechanism remain unresolved. Native abstract verifier: VERIFIED.

  • DOI:10.1073/pnas.1512671112
    Some swarms did merge, and most interestingly, this behavior was only seen in the most highly related strain pairs.

    Stefanic et al. 2015, PMID:26438858, PMC4653157; directly retrieved scientific abstract from Europe PMC with the namespace-qualified query EXT_ID:26438858 AND SRC:MED. Title, PMID and DOI were checked together. The authors define a conspecific scope and report relatedness-associated swarm merging versus boundary formation among sympatric Bacillus subtilis isolates. The separately reported root-colonization outcome is not a direct killing measurement. Full text, figures and supplements were inaccessible and are not claimed as inspected; indexed search passages were not used as excerpts or quantitative evidence. Native abstract verifier: VERIFIED. Linked PMID:26512099 is commentary, not a correction or experimental replication.

  • DOI:10.1128/mBio.00723-17
    The effects of kin discrimination extend to nearby species, as the other subtilis clade species were treated with the same antagonism as nonkin.

    Lyons and Kolter 2017, PMID:28679746, PMC5573675; scientific abstract, NLM version 1 PDF page 1 and JATS article-meta abstract without abstract-type. This is not the separate IMPORTANCE summary. Introduction distinguishes preferential kin association from nonkin exclusion and supports the definition's two modes; the paper's own experiments support the nearby-species extension. The comparison concerns interactions with B. subtilis NCIB3610: nearby species can be antagonized, while more distant lineages show varied outcomes. This is not a universal monotonic relation between distance and antagonism. Relatedness uses type-strain 16S, not whole-genome identity of every isolate. Swarm, biofilm and halo assays differ in medium and endpoint. Figure 2A and 2C assign WNC032 to different species; Data Set S1 identifies it as B. megaterium. No silent panel correction or taxon anchor is inferred. Actual Figures 2 and 4 and selected spreadsheet rows/totals were inspected, not all figures or raw assays. Native abstract verifier: VERIFIED. Shares an investigator with 2015; not an independent-laboratory replication.

Stefanic 2015 defines conspecific discrimination, whereas Lyons 2017 explicitly extends exclusion to nearby species. The record retains that attributed extension without a universal kin threshold, obligate killing, cognition or monotonic distance-response rule. Kin recognition, allorecognition and self/nonself discrimination are not asserted as exact synonyms. An interaction phenotype is required; a recognition locus, allele match, isolated pairing or spatial assortment alone is insufficient. Recognition-allele specificity, measured relatedness proxies and genome-wide relatedness must not be conflated. METPO:1000059 phenotype is the conservative parent. Mutualism (traitmech:000041) requires reciprocal benefit; biofilm formation (traitmech:000053) is neither necessary nor sufficient. Contact-dependent outer membrane exchange (traitmech:000650) is material transfer, and its existing negative kin-discrimination evidence remains a boundary, not a parent gap. Heterokaryon incompatibility (traitmech:000606) concerns postfusion restriction and is not automatically equivalent or reparented. Predation requires trophic benefit; DNA self/nonself defense has a different target and endpoint. No organism-level disjointness is implied.

Resolve strain, assay and source gaps before assigning canonical taxa or molecular mechanisms.
