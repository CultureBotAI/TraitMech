stalk contraction

traitmech:000730 · CLASS · PROPOSED

A physiological phenotype in which a microbial cell actively reduces the end-to-end extension of its stalk.

Trait evidence (2)

  • DOI:10.2142/biophysics.8.1
    The stalk is straight at the initial stage. Then, it bends and coils into a helix near the zooid.

    Kamiguri et al. 2012, PMID:27857602, PMCID:PMC5070450, printed page 3, Results and Discussion first paragraph, left column.   • DOI:10.1016/j.bpj.2012.07.038
    The stalk contraction was stalled more as the fluid viscosity and/or flow rate increased.

    Ryu et al. 2012, PMID:23009835, PMCID:PMC3433611, printed page 862, Figure 2A caption, sentence beginning The stalk contraction. All eight main-paper pages and actual Figures 1-5 were inspected; the supplement was not. Projection and geometry assumptions remain relevant. Some extracted micrometre symbols appear as mm; no such numeric extraction is quoted. Culture/taxonomy correspondence is unresolved. The measured endpoint is reduced end-to-end stalk extension, not necessarily reduced contour length. Coiling is observed, not a universal geometric requirement. Require active cellular contraction; static stalk morphology, reduced developmental growth, passive bending, detachment and isometric force alone do not establish it. Stalk-induced body displacement is not cell-body contraction traitmech:000728, and the endpoint differs from rapid axopodial contraction traitmech:000682. The direct parent is cell body or cytoplasmic projection contraction traitmech:000729, by definition-based inference. Amos's publisher abstract places the spasmoneme within cytoplasm and a sheath. The stalk is composite; its whole sheath is not asserted to be cytoplasm. Neither locomotion nor a universal ATP-driven motor is required by this parent choice. No exact synonym or organism-level disjointness is asserted.
