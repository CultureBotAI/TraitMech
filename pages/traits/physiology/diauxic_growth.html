diauxic growth

traitmech:000704 · CLASS · PROPOSED

A physiological phenotype in which a microbial population exhibits two successive growth phases associated with a switch from preferential use of one nutrient source to use of another as the first becomes limiting.

Trait evidence (5)

    Boulineau et al. 2013, PMID:23637881, PMC3640066; scientific abstract.   • DOI:10.1128/jb.164.2.866-871.1985

    George et al. 1985, PMID:3863813, PMC214331; directly retrieved source-qualified MED abstract only. Acetate preference in this experiment contradicts a universal glucose-first requirement and supports the lexical name diauxie. Full paper and figures were not inspected; a full-text XML endpoint returned HTTP 500. This access failure is not evidence that full text does not exist.

  • DOI:10.1128/jb.01571-06
    Following recovery of growth on the alternative carbon or nitrogen source, RpoS protein concentrations declined rapidly to a basal level.

    Sandercock and Page 2008, PMID:18055600, PMC2223558; scientific abstract. Relevant Methods/Results and actual Figure 3 support UW batch-culture acetate/glucose and ammonium/N2 transitions. This supports nutrient-source scope rather than sugars or carbon alone. Ammonium augmentation addresses an oxygen-limitation alternative for the stress response. RpoS dynamics are not a universal causal mechanism of diauxie; later PHB consumption was explicitly unmeasured. Publisher text and Figure 3 were accessible despite requests HTML 403 and Europe PMC XML 500 responses.

  • DOI:10.1371/journal.pbio.1002041
    many natural isolates of Saccharomyces cerevisiae display short or nonexistent diauxic lags when grown in mixtures of glucose (preferred) and galactose.

    Wang et al. 2015, PMID:25626068, PMC4308108; scientific abstract, not the XML's preceding TOC teaser or separate Author Summary. Relevant Results, growth-curve Methods, actual Figure 1 and S1 EPS were inspected. Operational zero lag means the smoothed growth-rate curve has no local minimum, not simultaneous equal use of both substrates. Natural-origin growth panels and engineered reporter derivatives require separate provenance. Figure 1 caption says eleven strains but the image has twelve labels; no strain count is inferred here. The observations oppose mandatory positive lag.

  • DOI:10.1038/msb.2013.11

    Zampar et al. 2013, PMID:23549479, PMC3693829; Introduction, second paragraph, not an abstract quote. Relevant Results, culture and measurement Methods and actual Figure 1 were inspected. FY4 was initially supplied glucose and later consumed produced ethanol; the definition therefore does not require both nutrients to be supplied initially. Figure 1 distinguishes concentration observations from fitted rates. Intracellular flux ranges are model-constrained, not direct flux measurements; raw supplemental data and the full mechanistic analysis were not reanalyzed.

Boulineau's Introduction defines sequential carbon use, while lag-emphasizing accounts operationalize a narrower growth-curve pattern. Wang reports short or nonexistent lags; Sandercock and Page extend the observed scope to nitrogen-source transitions. The present definition uses the nutrient-switching sense and does not require glucose preference, a positive minimum lag, complete arrest of every cell or strictly zero secondary-substrate use. An arbitrary biphasic growth curve without nutrient-switching evidence is insufficient. Diauxic shift denotes the transition; polyauxie and unqualified diphasic growth are not exact synonyms. METPO:1000731 nutrient adaptation is a trophic-type class whose local record operationalizes chronic copiotroph/oligotroph nutrient regimes, not this temporal pattern. Phenotype is the conservative parent pending upstream hierarchy review. Generalist and specialist denote niche breadth; dormancy, carbon catabolite repression and generic resource-use predicates are not exact equivalents.
