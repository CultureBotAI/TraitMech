cell-body contraction

traitmech:000728 · CLASS · PROPOSED

A physiological phenotype in which a microbial cell actively shortens its body by contraction of intracellular structures.

Trait evidence (3)

  • DOI:10.1126/sciadv.add6550
    Ca2+ concentrations from 10−6 to 10−2 M in the culture medium could stimulate cell contraction while maintaining the viability of the cell.

    Zhang et al. 2023, PMID:36812318, PMCID:PMC9946354, Results: functional analysis of giant backbone proteins, second paragraph. Direct JATS match; native NOT_IN_ABSTRACT retained. The study identified its East Lake, Wuhan isolate as Spirostomum minus using morphology, protargol staining and SSU rDNA, then established clonal cultures at 22 C. Actual Figure 1A shows extended and contracted cell bodies. The quoted treatment maintained viability; 38-to-16 seconds describes intervals between contractions, not contraction duration. Selected Results, Methods and Discussion, actual Figures 1, 6 and 9, and relevant supplement pages were inspected. Figure 9 is a mechanistic schematic. Supplementary Figure 7 shows shortening during death, which is insufficient for this trait. Abnormal-cell counts are in Supplementary Table 2, not Table S1 as cited in the RNAi Results. Rare twisted RNAi phenotypes do not establish contraction loss in every treated cell. The source's superscript exponents mean 10^(-6) to 10^(-2) M; the normalized plain-text quotation does not preserve superscript styling.

  • DOI:10.1073/pnas.2217737120
    Asymmetric contraction can be observed as the top end begins contracting before the bottom end

    Floyd et al. 2023, PMID:37307463, PMCID:PMC10288572, Figure 4 caption, contiguous observed-asymmetry clause. Direct JATS match; native NOT_IN_ABSTRACT retained. The quoted top and bottom ends are those of the imaged Spirostomum cell body in Figure 4A-C, not Vorticella's stalk or simulated springs. The actual panel A contours and panel B solid length trajectory show body shortening; panel C shows the staggered endpoint movements. Dashed model curves are separate from observations. The 15-word caption span replaces the data-presentation excerpt rejected in #1935; historical source receipts remain in the sidecar. Actual Figures 1A and 2A-B show Spirostomum body shortening and experimental trajectories separately from fitted curves. Methods used commercially sourced cultures and high-speed imaging without compressing cells; no species identity is inferred. Supplementary Figure S3 measures length along the central backbone curve, with increasing width. Supplementary Figure S6 states that repeated contractions of the same organism were not distinguished from contractions of different organisms: five traces are not five established biological replicates. Vorticella panels measure stalk contraction, not the body endpoint. Calcium-wave and energy models are not universal molecular proof. Selected experimental sections, actual Figures 1-2 and supplementary pages 3 and 5 were inspected; raw videos and the complete model were not reanalyzed.

  • DOI:10.1242/jeb.189.1.163
    Living Vorticella showed a graded shrinkage of the cell body when Ca2+ buffer was injected into the cell body

    Katoh and Naitoh 1994, PMID:9317557, scientific Abstract item 3, first clause. Direct namespace-qualified Europe PMC MED abstract match and native VERIFIED. The same sentence contrasts graded cell-body shrinkage with all-or-nothing stalk coiling. Triton-extracted preparations in other abstract items are not the living-cell injection assay. Calcium-release causality is presented as an assumption, not direct molecular proof. Publisher HTML and PDF requests returned 403; full Methods, figures and strain provenance were not accessed. No species-wide example, quantitative threshold or universal trigger is inferred.

Retain phenotype METPO:1000059. Motility METPO:1000701 concerns locomotion through the environment; motile METPO:1000702 specifies independent movement using metabolic energy. Neither necessarily establishes body shortening. Body contraction is not displacement caused only by stalk coiling, rapid axopodial contraction traitmech:000682, contractile-vacuole cycling, injection-system contraction, cytokinetic constriction, passive osmotic shrinkage, death or static short-cell size. Behavioral habituation traitmech:000714 changes response probability rather than defining the contraction response. The axopodial record's broader cellular-contractility parent question is not resolved by this body-specific class; do not reparent it here. No universal speed, fractional shortening, trigger or ecological function is imposed. No organism-level disjointness or exact synonym for generic contractility is asserted.
