carbon-source memory

traitmech:000706 · CLASS · PROPOSED

A physiological phenotype in which a non-genetic cellular state established by prior carbon-source conditions persists after those conditions change and alters subsequent induction or growth responses to carbon sources.

Trait evidence (3)

  • DOI:10.1371/journal.pgen.1004556
    Cells grown under cyclical glucose/lactose conditions thus displayed phenotypic memory of their previous metabolic adaptation.

    Lambert and Kussell 2014, PMID:25255314, PMC4177670; Results / Phenotypic memory in response to sudden environmental changes, not the abstract. Primary text, actual Figure 2 and Text S1 were inspected. Repeated glucose/lactose switches demonstrate history-dependent growth; the paper distinguishes inherited protein persistence from continuing induction after stimulus removal. F3 is an engineered W3110 derivative, not an unmodified natural exemplar. Growth is a chamber-level relative elongation readout. DOI:10.1371/journal.pgen.1004793 corrects author-name typesetting, not the biological claims; the correction was read. Model predictions are not additional experimental replication. Other figures, raw data and equations were not independently reanalyzed.

  • DOI:10.7554/eLife.39234
    past exposure to an environmental condition can shorten the time needed to adapt when the condition re-occurs

    Cerulus et al. 2018, PMID:30299256, PMC6211830; scientific abstract, confirmed against NCBI full-text XML. Relevant Results, Methods, Discussion, actual Figures 1, 2, 6 and Figure 1 supplement 4 were inspected. Carbon-shift assays separate population lag from single-cell growth resumption; cells not resuming within the observation window are not thereby proved dead. Cross-sugar effects and respiratory perturbations oppose an obligatory same-sugar or sugar-specific protein-inheritance mechanism. The main AN63 series is engineered; the separately reported non-laboratory strains have unreviewed primary provenance here. Publisher article access was challenged; public figure assets were accessible. Raw data and all other supplements were not reanalyzed.

  • DOI:10.1016/j.cub.2007.10.044
    This "galactose memory" persists for several generations

    Zacharioudakis et al. 2007, PMID:17997309; directly retrieved, source-qualified MED scientific abstract only. The preceding sentence compares initial GAL activation with rapid reactivation in progeny after intervening glucose growth. This supports the induction endpoint, not a measured growth-rate benefit. Its Gal1 and cytoplasmic-factor interpretation remains assay-specific; the abstract's chromatin conclusion is not universalized. Full text, figures and strain provenance were not inspected. The index links a Comment in, not a correction or independent replication.
