cell body or cytoplasmic projection contraction

traitmech:000729 · CLASS · PROPOSED

A physiological phenotype in which a microbial cell actively contracts its body or a cytoplasmic projection, reducing its length or extension.

Trait evidence (3)

  • DOI:10.1242/jeb.189.1.163
    Living Vorticella showed a graded shrinkage of the cell body when Ca2+ buffer was injected into the cell body

    Katoh and Naitoh 1994, PMID:9317557, scientific Abstract item 3, first clause. Direct namespace-qualified Europe PMC MED match; native VERIFIED. The same sentence contrasts graded cell-body shrinkage with all-or-nothing stalk coiling. This supports the curator-synthesized body/projection-shortening parent, not a universal calcium threshold. Other abstract items use Triton-extracted preparations, not the living-cell injection assay. Calcium-induced calcium release is an assumption, not direct molecular proof. Publisher requests returned 403; full Methods, figures and strain provenance were not accessed.

  • DOI:10.2108/zsj.20.1367
    Axopodial contraction of the centrohelid heliozoon Raphidiophrys contractilis was induced by mechanical or electrical stimulation.

    Khan et al. 2003, PMID:14624035, scientific Abstract opening sentence. Direct namespace-qualified Europe PMC MED match; native VERIFIED. This supports stimulated shortening of a cytoplasmic projection rather than whole-cell locomotion. Reported calcium dependence and speed apply to this assay, not every member of the parent class. Non-detection of other filaments after fixation is not proof of their absence. Full Methods and figures were not accessed; the publisher's HTTP 200 response was an access challenge, not readable article content. Current taxonomic and culture identities remain unresolved.

  • DOI:10.1083/jcb.118.3.585
    Both recording techniques showed that the rapid contraction involves 80% of the total cell length.

    Febvre-Chevalier and Febvre 1992, PMID:1639845, PMCID:PMC2289557, Results, Kinetics of the Contraction, printed page 588, right column. Direct extracted-PDF match with the typeset passage visually checked; native NOT_IN_ABSTRACT retained. The primary PDF was retrieved from its Semantic Scholar mirror after official PDF attempts failed. Selected Methods/Results and actual Figures 1 and 3-6 were inspected. Length was measured from head top to stalk base, not isolated cell-body length; graph axes use stalk length. The percentage is study-specific. Natural-seawater responses differ from artificially slowed responses. The specimens were collected near Villefranche and cultured at 14-18 C; current taxonomic identity is not verified. The n=20 initial-length sample is not asserted as independent contraction replication. Microtubule disassembly accompanies shortening, but this study expressly does not establish force production. Its disassembly half-time is not automatically contraction duration. The accessible JATS contains an abstract and PDF pointer, not the complete experimental text.

Require active reduction of cell-body length or the extension of a cytoplasmic projection. Generic force production or isometric contractility alone is insufficient. Extracellular pilus retraction, injection-system contraction, contractile-vacuole cycling and cytokinetic constriction alone do not establish this endpoint. Neither passive osmotic shrinkage, death-associated shortening nor static short-cell morphology suffices. The existing body and rapid axopodial traits are narrower children; stalk contraction traitmech:000730 is now a narrower child, defined by active reduction of stalk end-to-end extension. This does not require shortening of the stalk contour or make its sheath cytoplasm. Motility METPO:1000701 and motile METPO:1000702 concern locomotion, not necessarily shortening. Active denotes the cellular response, not demonstrated ATP-driven motor action. No universal speed, fractional shortening, trigger, calcium threshold, ATP dependence, ecological role, exact synonym for generic contractility or organism-level disjointness is asserted.
