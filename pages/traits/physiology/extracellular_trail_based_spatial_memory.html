extracellular-trail-based spatial memory

traitmech:000715 · CLASS · PROPOSED

A physiological phenotype in which a microorganism's subsequent spatial exploration is guided by persistent extracellular trails deposited during earlier movement.

Trait evidence (3)

  • DOI:10.1073/pnas.1215037109
    When one arm contained extracellular slime, 39 of 40 plasmodia chose the blank agar arm

    Reid et al. 2012, PMID:23045640, PMC3491460; Results opening paragraph, printed page 17490 / PDF page 1. Cultured Physarum polycephalum avoided previously deposited slime in a Y maze. Same-stock coatings do not demonstrate individual recognition of one's own trace. In the separate U-trap experiment, 23/24 plasmodia on blank agar versus 8/24 on slime-coated agar reached the target within 120 hours. Coating masks newly informative trails; it does not abolish secretion or lesion internal memory. Success, completion time among successful trials, speed, path length and revisitation are distinct endpoints. No-trap and speed controls do not exclude every environmental confound. Main Methods, Results, Discussion and all four figures were inspected in an author-hosted publisher-formatted PDF bearing a 2012 download stamp, not verified as the current corrected VoR. Supplements and raw data were not assessed. Southern Biological Supplies culture provenance remains unresolved. The contiguous result span was text-matched and visually checked.

  • DOI:10.1088/1361-6463/aa87df
    Our results suggest that the external memory has an adaptive advantage in ‘open’ and simple bounded environments.

    Smith-Ferguson et al. 2017; scientific abstract, printed page 1 / PDF page 2. This is the authors' interpretation of maze performance, not a measured fitness or natural-selection endpoint. Within 72 hours, blank versus extracellular-slime conditions yielded success in 24/24 versus 9/24 open trials, 12/15 versus 4/12 simple-maze trials and 11/17 versus 11/13 complex-maze trials; the last comparison showed no significant benefit. Completion times conditional on success were not significantly different. Escaped trials were excluded; split decisions used the dominant front. Printed page 6 pairs the same chi-square 0.802 with P=0.004 and P=0.371; this discrepancy is unresolved. Figure 8 is illustrative, not quantified trajectory evidence. Main Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusion and all eight figures were inspected; supplements were not. The author-hosted PDF bears a 2017 download stamp, not verified current VoR equivalence. This study shares authors with Reid 2012, not an independent-laboratory replication.

  • DOI:10.1073/pnas.2312611121
    In accordance with the literature, we observe that plasmodia generally avoid previously visited areas marked by their slime trail

    Troger et al. 2024, PMID:38517977, PMC10990088; Results, Quantifying Run-and-Tumble and Self-Avoiding Behavior, page 3. The authors report their own Physarum observations, not only a background citation. Actual trajectories, including Figure 2A, are distinct from simulated-model panels. Page 5 describes 14 trajectories divided into groups of nine each; Figure 4 instead gives seven small and seven large. The discrepancy remains unresolved. The 0.65 square-millimeter median is a cohort split, not a universal threshold. Small plasmodia can cross trails; a modeled three-hour forgetting time is not a measured chemical half-life or internal-memory duration. Main text, Methods and all four main figures were inspected in the author-hosted publisher-formatted PDF with a March 2024 download stamp, not verified current corrected VoR equivalence. Publisher supplements and raw data were not reanalyzed. This different laboratory's assay is not a replication of the earlier maze experiments; no universal molecular receptor is established.

This operational phenotype concerns spatial information persisting outside the microorganism. It does not require always avoiding a trail, improved navigation in every setting, individual self-recognition, or internal recall. Carbon-source memory (traitmech:000706), acquired stress-resistance memory (traitmech:000712), enhanced transcriptional reactivation memory (traitmech:000713) and behavioral habituation (traitmech:000714) have different endpoints. Negative autotropism (traitmech:000603) concerns germ-tube or hyphal orientation. Collective bacterial predation (traitmech:000701) concerns coordinated killing and feeding, despite a trail mention. Contact-dependent outer membrane exchange (traitmech:000650) concerns actual intercellular exchange. METPO:1000059 phenotype is the conservative parent pending hierarchy review; local chemotaxis has a flagellar-specific definition and is not a universal parent. Generic memory, learning, stigmergy and extracellular polymer production are not exact synonyms. Psl-biased visitation and community growth history require separate scope and evidence assessments.
