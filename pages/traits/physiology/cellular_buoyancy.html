cellular buoyancy

traitmech:000528 · CLASS · PROPOSED

A physiology trait in which intracellular gas vesicles reduce a microbial cell's effective density enough to provide buoyancy and vertical positioning in the water column.

Trait evidence (3)

  • DOI:10.1186/s13036-024-00426-3
    can provide buoyancy for them to move up and down the water to obtain nutrients

    Feng et al. review gas-vesicle physical properties that provide buoyancy for photosynthetic bacteria and halophilic archaea.

  • DOI:10.1186/s13036-024-00426-3
    give them the buoyancy to move upward so that they can get enough light for photosynthesis

    Feng et al. describe gas vesicles giving photosynthetic bacteria buoyancy to move upward toward sufficient light.

  • DOI:10.3390/life12091455
    Approximately 3–10% of the cell volume must be occupied by gas vesicles to provide buoyancy

    Pfeifer reviews gas-vesicle proteins and summarizes the fraction of cell volume that must contain gas vesicles to confer buoyancy.
