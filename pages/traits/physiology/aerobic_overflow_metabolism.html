aerobic overflow metabolism

traitmech:000705 · CLASS · PROPOSED

A physiological phenotype in which microbial cells divert part of their catabolic carbon flux to excreted, incompletely oxidized products with lower ATP yield per substrate than complete respiration, despite possessing respiratory capacity and sufficient oxygen for respiration.

Trait evidence (3)

  • DOI:10.1038/msb.2009.82
    S. cerevisiae and E. coli fully oxidize glucose at low concentrations and shift to overflow metabolism at high glucose concentrations.

    Molenaar et al. 2009, PMID:19888218, PMC2795476; Current growth models and their limitations, not the abstract. The surrounding paragraph supplies the fully aerobic, incomplete-oxidation and retained respiratory-capacity differentia. This Perspective contains original modeling, not new experimental replication of the cited organisms. The terminology discussion also includes mixed-acid-to-lactate shifts; the qualified label here does not define every use of unqualified overflow metabolism.

  • DOI:10.1038/nature15765
    cells use fermentation instead of the more efficient respiration to generate energy, despite the availability of oxygen.

    Basan et al. 2015, PMID:26632588, PMC4843128; scientific abstract. Relevant Results, growth Methods, model scope, Extended Data Table 1, actual Figure 1 and Extended Data Figure 2 were inspected through public XML and figure assets. Acetate excretion depends on growth conditions; the fitted threshold is not universal. The oxidative-fermentation accounting includes oxygen-dependent NADH oxidation. Net excretion does not exclude acetate reuptake. Proteome allocation is a tested model in this system, not a necessary mechanism for every member of the trait. Complete supplements, equations and referenced batch-culture methods were not independently reanalyzed. Publisher retrieval failed; the public full-text XML was accessible.

  • DOI:10.1111/febs.13019
    All fermenting yeasts exhibited significant ethanol formation within the first 20 min in response to a glucose pulse

    Hagman et al. 2014, PMID:25161062, PMC4240471; Results and Discussion / Consumption of glucose and generation of biomass and ethanol, not the abstract. Introduction, relevant Results, Experimental procedures and actual Figures 1-2 were inspected. Glucose pulses into initially respiratory cultures produced ethanol in responsive yeasts while dissolved oxygen was controlled above 30% air saturation. The authors report no first-hour repression of respiration; Figure 2 does not establish monotonic or unchanged oxygen-consumption rates. Replication excludes V. polyspora; E. coryli received supplementation. This short-term Crabtree assay is not an exact synonym for the entire microbial phenotype. Supplemental time series and strain origins were not fully reviewed.

The qualifier aerobic is deliberate. Unqualified overflow, Crabtree effect, respiro-fermentation and industrial aerobic fermentation are not asserted as exact synonyms. The local METPO:1002005 fermentation record is donor/acceptor-defined and excludes external electron acceptors; Basan's oxidative branch uses different accounting. https://github.com/berkeleybop/metpo/issues/468 discusses fermentation hierarchy, and the pinned ontology already differs from the retained local parent/definition. Neither that process nor METPO:1000060 metabolism is adopted as an is-a parent of this organismal phenotype. METPO:1000059 phenotype is used pending upstream hierarchy review. Product-specific ethanol/butanediol fermentation, aerotolerance, facultative anaerobiosis, copiotrophy and diauxic growth are not equivalent. Product detection or oxygen presence alone is insufficient: oxygen limitation must be excluded. No universal substrate, product, concentration, growth-rate threshold, complete respiratory shutdown or fixed molecular mechanism is required.
