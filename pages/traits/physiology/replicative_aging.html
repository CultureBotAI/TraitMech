replicative aging

traitmech:000709 · CLASS · PROPOSED

A physiological phenotype in which microbial cells exhibit declining growth or reproductive performance, or increasing mortality risk, with increasing replicative age under specified conditions.

Trait evidence (4)

  • DOI:10.1098/rstb.2018.0442
    For both cell types, division time progressively increased over successive generations

    Lapinska et al. 2019, PMID:31587633, PMC6792439; Figure 2 caption, directly checked in primary full-text XML and the author-hosted PDF. The Introduction defines aging by declining performance. Figure 2 was visually inspected: the early division-time trend approaches a plateau in the longer run, not indefinite deterioration. Methods separate brightfield aging measurements from fluorescent uptake/aggregation assays. Methods name BW25113, but the Introduction says BW21113; that discrepancy is unresolved. Starting-condition effects remain a limitation. No universal protein mechanism is established.

  • DOI:10.1186/1471-2148-7-126
    Aging refers to a decline in reproduction and survival with increasing age.

    Ackermann et al. 2007, PMID:17662151, PMC2174458; scientific abstract Background, directly checked against MED and primary full-text XML. Caulobacter stalked-cell cohorts showed age-dependent loss of reproductive function, with both faster and slower aging among evolved clones. This endpoint combines survival and reproduction and cannot separate them. UJ590 is a pilA-deletion derivative, not an unmodified natural exemplar. Its elapsed-time assay complements, rather than replaces, the division-indexed evidence above.

  • DOI:10.7554/eLife.20340
    Surprisingly, cells die without the classic hallmarks of cellular aging, such as progressive changes in size, doubling time, or sibling health.

    Spivey et al. 2017, PMID:28139976, PMC5332158; scientific abstract, not the eLife digest, directly checked against MED and primary full-text XML. Boundary evidence: finite replicative lifespan and its extension do not alone demonstrate age-dependent deterioration. The reported age-independent mortality applies to the measured fission-yeast conditions, not all strains and environments. The abstract and Discussion were assessed, not all experiments or figure images.

  • DOI:10.1371/journal.pbio.3002245
    This occurs without an increase in lifespan, proving that at least in yeast, lifespan is distinct from senescence and loss of fitness.

    Horkai et al. 2023, PMID:37643155, PMC10464975; Results, Suppression of age-linked phenotypic change, directly checked against publisher text and primary XML. Galactose-aged diploid MEP cells retained better performance without longer lifespan. This engineered mother-enrichment system and its platform-dependent results are not universal natural-taxon claims. The authors also use replicative ageing for passage through divisions without senescent decline; that broader usage is not the operational phenotype defined here.

Replicative age follows a cell or inherited cell lineage across successive divisions; culture age alone is not that measurement. Use age-resolved performance or mortality risk, not merely a finite lifespan, old-pole identity, endpoint death, cumulative survival loss or an old/new sibling difference. A decline can approach a plateau; continuous worsening until death is not required. The record uses the functional-decline sense of aging, while Horkai et al. 2023 explicitly distinguish senescence from broader replicative ageing. Upstream review should assess that label boundary; no exact synonym or external xref is asserted. Chronological survival in nondividing cultures, population-wide clonal senescence and irreversible mammalian cell-cycle arrest are not equated with this phenotype. The existing gram-variable culture_aging node denotes stationary-phase conditions and is not an exact grounding target. METPO:1000059 phenotype is the conservative broader class; no existing record is reparented.
