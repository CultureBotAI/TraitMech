reversible nematode-paralyzing activity

traitmech:000722 · CLASS · PROPOSED

A physiological capability of a microorganism to induce a loss of active nematode movement that reverses after exposure to the microorganism or its products ends.

Trait evidence (2)

  • https://journals.flvc.org/nematropica/article/view/64427
    Bioassays revealed that filtrates of P. lilacinus and Paecilomyces marquandii caused reversible paralysis of male and juvenile R. similis.

    Kilama et al. 2007, English Abstract, printed page 215/PDF page 1. Direct publisher HTML/PDF and typeset match; native verifier skips URL references, not VERIFIED. Needle-probed immobilization at 2 h was followed by rinsing and reassessment labelled 21 h; Discussion says 21 h after rinsing. Bioassay 2 lacks recovery testing; bioassay 3 has a high-paralysis pH control and bioassay 5 is null against controls. Actual Figure 1 measures initial paralysis; Results/Table 2 address recovery. Repeated counts are not individual trajectories. No universal host/stage response or dose threshold is inferred.

  • https://horizon.documentation.ird.fr/exl-doc/pleins_textes/pleins_textes_5/pt5/nemato/27861.pdf
    Secondly, the majority of larvae are able to recover the mobility in fresh water following all treatment times up to 48 h.

    Cayrol, Djian and Pijarowski 1989, Results, Study of the reversibility of the toxic effect, printed page 335/PDF page 5; actual Figure 3 inspected. This recovery result concerns M. arenaria larvae exposed to Brazilian P. lilacinus culture filtrate: 10-day motionless, aerated, illuminated malt culture, 15 g/L, undiluted filtrate, varied exposure, then 24 h in water. Needle probing and water/pH-matched medium controls are described. Summary says less than 48 h, Results through 48 h; no universal threshold is adopted. Typeset all is quoted; PDF text extraction misreads it as dl. Native URL verification is skipped. This is recovery support, not a standalone producer-identity excerpt or proof of a neural mechanism.
