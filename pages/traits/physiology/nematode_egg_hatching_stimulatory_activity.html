nematode egg-hatching stimulatory activity

traitmech:000724 · CLASS · PROPOSED

A physiological capability of a microorganism to increase nematode egg hatching over a defined observation interval relative to comparable controls without that microbial influence.

Trait evidence (2)

  • DOI:10.1163/156854104323072883
    Fifteen isolates (5.9%) produced compounds that strongly stimulated SCN egg hatch compared to PDB controls (Table 1).

    Meyer et al. 2004, Results, printed page 28/PDF page 6, lower left. SCN denotes Heterodera glycines; PDB is potato dextrose broth. Native UNRESOLVED; Crossref and the USDA-hosted article verify identity. The typeset span was visually checked; PDFKit splits controls across lines, so plain whitespace normalization does not match. Hatch counts include motile and immotile juveniles at 14 days, normalized to initial eggs and then uninoculated PDB hatch. Three or seven days denotes culture age, not egg exposure. The source-specific strong-effect threshold combines effect size and statistical significance; it is not a universal trait cutoff. Forty isolates were retested, not all 253. Nutrient depletion versus added stimulants remains unresolved despite the quote's compound wording. pH was measured, not neutralized. Host, isolate and culture age can change effect direction.

  • DOI:10.1186/s13071-015-0986-z
    When incubated with Gram-positive bacteria, only T. muris eggs showed noticeable hatching after 20 h, although with high variability.

    Vejzagic et al. 2015, PMID:26174801, PMCID:PMC4501204, scientific Abstract Results; exact normalized publisher/XML match and native VERIFIED. This is its own bacterial experiment, not a citation of Meyer. Full Results and actual Figure 2 show Enterococcus caccae and Lactobacillus reuteri above bacteria-free controls after 20 h without antimicrobials, with substantial within- and between-run variability. Hatch is released larvae divided by initial embryonated eggs. T. muris and T. suis are Trichuris muris and Trichuris suis; little T. suis hatch in these conditions is not absolute inability. Antimicrobials and egg storage history affect interpretation. The E. coli strain/concentration ANOVA excludes near-zero controls; that analysis is not a statistical contrast against no bacteria. No direct-contact mechanism is inferred from background citations.
