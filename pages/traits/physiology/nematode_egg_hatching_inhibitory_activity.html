nematode egg-hatching inhibitory activity

traitmech:000723 · CLASS · PROPOSED

A physiological capability of a microorganism to reduce nematode egg hatching over a defined observation interval relative to comparable unexposed controls.

Trait evidence (2)

  • DOI:10.1163/156854104323072883
    The least hatches were recorded in 7-day-old C. globosum (L250) filtrate, with 85 and 98% hatch reductions for SCN and RKN, respectively.

    Meyer et al. 2004, Results, printed page 28/PDF page 6; all ten pages and actual Figure 1/Table 1 inspected. C. globosum is Chaetomium globosum; SCN and RKN are Heterodera glycines and Meloidogyne incognita. Native UNRESOLVED; Crossref and USDA verify identity. Typeset span matches; PDF extraction loses the fi ligature. Seven days is culture age, not egg exposure: hatch was scored after 14 days, including motile and immotile juveniles. Percent hatch uses initial eggs, then uninoculated PDB hatch as comparator; quoted reductions are relative to that control, not egg mortality. Biomass-free filtrate concentrations differ between hosts (75/90%); approximately 200 eggs per well, five wells per treatment, water viability control. Forty isolates were retested, not all 253. pH was measured, not neutralized; no identified active molecule or universal potency threshold is inferred.

  • DOI:10.1038/s41598-023-33669-z
    T. asperellum FbMi6 inhibited egg hatching in all concentrations as compared to control.

    Saharan et al. 2023, PMID:37088805, Results; direct publisher HTML match, native NOT_IN_ABSTRACT. This is its own experiment, not its background account of Meyer. Five surface-sterilized egg masses received spore-checked culture filtrates at 20/40/60/80%, with water and PDB controls; alternate-day counts through ten days, two repeats at 25 +/- 2 degrees C. Actual Figure 3 inspected. Results associates maximum inhibition with 120 h, whereas its legend specifies 2/4/6/8/10 days; no exact maximum/time pairing is adopted. Egg deformation does not prove death. Juvenile water-recovery findings are not transferred to eggs.
