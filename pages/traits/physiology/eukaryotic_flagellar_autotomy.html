eukaryotic flagellar autotomy

traitmech:000731 · CLASS · PROPOSED

A physiological phenotype in which a microbial eukaryote sheds its cilia or flagella by regulated severing of their axonemes near the base.

Trait evidence (2)

  • DOI:10.1186/1471-2121-4-11
    fla10 cells lose flagella by deflagellation at 33 degrees C.

    Parker and Quarmby 2003, PMID:12930563, PMCID:PMC194436, scientific abstract Results, second sentence. Direct MED text match; native VERIFIED applies to that text, not the PDF degree-symbol typography. Background distinguishes basal axonemal severing from resorption. The quoted response concerns source-named Chlamydomonas fla10 in calcium-containing medium, not reference strain WTB214. Released flagella were counted per 100 cells, with the initial count subtracted; lengths are a separate endpoint. Low calcium is not calcium-free, and buffer exchange is a confound. All twelve main PDF pages and eight figures were inspected. No universal IFT or calcium requirement follows.

  • DOI:10.1534/g3.116.034264
    Wild-type cells deflagellate completely in response to pH shock or dibucaine, regardless of the temperature

    Hilton et al. 2016, PMID:27520959, PMCID:PMC5068960.1, page 3416, Results and Discussion subsection ADF2 and ADF4 remain unassigned, first paragraph and Figure 4A. Contiguous JATS and typeset match; native NOT_IN_ABSTRACT retained. The comparison is 21 versus 33 degrees C after six hours, not every temperature. The assay scores the control-normalized decrease in biflagellate cells per 100 cells, not a direct count of released flagella or proof that every responding cell loses both. Figure 4A reports three independent experiments with SD. All ten main pages, four figures and three tables were inspected; supplements remain unread. Phototaxis screening alone is not the shedding endpoint.
