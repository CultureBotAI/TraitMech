behavioral habituation

traitmech:000714 · CLASS · PROPOSED

A physiological phenotype in which repeated stimulation produces an experience-dependent decrease in a microbial organism's behavioral response that is not attributable solely to sensory adaptation or motor fatigue.

Trait evidence (4)

  • DOI:10.1098/rspb.2016.0446
    The Q and CAF slime moulds crossing a bridge containing quinine or caffeine for the sixth time showed reduced aversive behaviour.

    Boisseau et al. 2016, PMID:27122563, PMC4855389; page 4, Results (b), Test phase 1. Q and CAF denote cultured Physarum polycephalum plasmodia repeatedly exposed to quinine or caffeine bridges. Five daily exposures precede same/alternate-repellent tests; two plain-agar days precede retesting. Contact latency, crossing time and shape are different readouts. Caffeine does not show every quinine effect: initial crossing time was not slower than control, and recovery was incomplete. Residual aversiveness remains. Alternate-repellent responses support stimulus specificity, not universal exclusion of every confound. Main Methods, Results, Discussion and all five figures were inspected in a public publisher-formatted PDF bearing a 2016 download stamp, not verified as the current corrected VoR. Supplementary statistics and raw data were not assessed. The contiguous quote crosses PDF columns; direct text and visual checks agree. Native abstract lookup: NOT_IN_ABSTRACT.

  • DOI:10.1016/j.cub.2025.05.071
    the response to the low force stimuli shows extensive habituation even though the response to the alternating high force stimuli does not.

    Rajan and Marshall 2025, PMID:40562036, PMC13499368; Figure 4F caption, concluding result clause in retained PMC author-manuscript HTML. Figure 4F is an experimental contraction response in cultured Stentor coeruleus, not the adjacent model panel. Preserved stronger-stimulus responses argue against general motor exhaustion. The caption describes identical control timing, while Methods describes one low-force control tap per minute versus alternating forces at one total tap per minute; exact cadence equivalence remains unresolved. Figure 4I uses intervening low-force stimulation, not a stimulus-free rest; Figure 5 separately tests recovery after training. Anchored healthy nondividing cells were selected from Ward's cultures; field-of-view denominators vary. Experimental Results, relevant Methods and actual Figures 4-7 were inspected. Receptor internalization is modeled, not an identified molecular mechanism. Supplements and simulations were not reanalyzed. Native abstract lookup: NOT_IN_ABSTRACT; the exact full-text caption match is a separate check.

  • DOI:10.1016/j.cub.2026.03.080
    For cells treated with either cycloheximide or puromycin, the primary effect on learning was accelerating habituation (Figure 1C, 1E).

    Rajan et al. 2026, PMID:42025171, PMC13192338; Results S3, retained PMC author manuscript. Stentor contraction assays compare drug-treated and vehicle cultures under repeated mechanical stimulation. Figure 1C uses puromycin with two-hour pretreatment; Figure 1E describes 12-hour cycloheximide pretreatment, unlike general short-term Methods prose. Dose, stimulus strength and cadence differ; conditions are not interchangeable. Drug effects do not prove universally protein-synthesis-independent learning. Relevant Results and Methods and actual Figures 1, 3, 4 and 6 were inspected, not all omics analyses or supplements. RNAi and pharmacology retain off-target, anchoring, feeding and subculture limitations. SteCoe_6763 recovery differences were not significant. Figure 6 follows division during continuing stimuli, not retention after withdrawal. Results and Methods point to different supplementary electrical-control panels. This follow-up shares a laboratory with the 2025 study, not an independent replication. Native abstract lookup: NOT_IN_ABSTRACT; directly matched Results quote.

  • DOI:10.1016/j.nlm.2008.09.012
    Habituation is defined as a behavioral response decrement that results from repeated stimulation and that does not involve sensory adaptation/sensory fatigue or motor fatigue.

    Rankin et al. 2009, PMID:18854219, PMC2754195; section 2, page 136 (PDF page 2), complete opening definition. This consensus review supports operational terminology, not a microbial experiment or independent replication. Its nervous-system framing is not imposed on non-neural microbes. The public institutional PDF was directly read and pages 2-4 visually inspected. PDF ligatures are expanded and whitespace folded for matching; no paraphrase is substituted. Partial recovery is allowed. Responding to a novel stimulus is not itself dishabituation, which restores the original response. The behavioral criteria are not a demonstrated common molecular mechanism. Source-qualified MED identity was checked; its absent commentCorrectionList is not exhaustive notice clearance.

Repeated-stimulus decrement alone is insufficient: evaluate sensory adaptation, motor fatigue, injury/toxicity, selection and depletion alternatives in the actual assay. Specificity and recovery provide operational support without identifying all underlying mechanisms. Rankin 2009 frames habituation in neural behavioral terms; Rajan 2025 distinguishes that usage from cellular adaptation while modeling related receptor dynamics. This record concerns microbial behavior, not a categorical ban on shared molecular machinery. No fixed time, curve, complete recovery, dishabituation or all ten hallmarks are required in every experiment. Response to a stronger stimulus is not itself dishabituation. Carbon-source memory (traitmech:000706), acquired stress-resistance memory (traitmech:000712), enhanced transcriptional reactivation memory (traitmech:000713), anticipation, bet hedging and nutrient adaptation have distinct endpoints. Chemotaxis is not a parent of every behavioral assay. METPO:1000059 phenotype is the conservative parent; generic learning, memory and adaptation are not asserted as exact synonyms.
