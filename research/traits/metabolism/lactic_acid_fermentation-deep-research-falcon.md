# Microbial Trait Causal Graph Curation Report: Lactic Acid Fermentation (traitmech:000026)

## 0. Executive scope summary
**Trait meaning (curation-ready):** *Lactic acid fermentation* is a fermentative energy-conserving metabolism in which sugars are catabolized with ATP generated by substrate-level phosphorylation and **lactate is a major end product**. Within lactic acid bacteria (LAB), it is commonly divided into **homolactic (homofermentative) fermentation**—primarily glycolysis/EMP with lactate as the dominant end product—and **heterolactic (heterofermentative) fermentation**—a phosphoketolase/pentose-phosphate–linked route yielding lactate plus CO2 and ethanol and/or acetate. (aguirregarcia2024lacticacidfermentation pages 2-4, hakim2023acomprehensivereview pages 5-6, christofi2023mixedcultureanaerobicacidogenic pages 15-19)

**Boundary/nearby traits (avoid conflation):**
- **Bifid shunt** (Actinobacteriota/Bifidobacterium-like metabolism) can yield lactate + acetate but is mechanistically distinct from classical LAB phosphoketolase heterofermentation; treat as a boundary case or separate trait/module unless explicitly intended. (walters2023ametagenomelevelanalysis pages 9-11)
- Industrial lactic acid production can be implemented in **non-LAB hosts** (e.g., engineered yeasts/fungi); these are *implementations of lactate production* but not necessarily evidence for the LAB trait mechanism. (melo2024justaroundthe pages 9-11)

A pathway overview figure distinguishing homo- vs heterofermentative routes (glycolysis/EMP vs phosphoketolase) is available in Aguirre-Garcia et al. 2024 (Figure 1). (aguirregarcia2024lacticacidfermentation media cf6c2638)

## 1. Key concepts and definitions (current understanding)
### 1.1 Homolactic vs heterolactic fermentation
- **Homofermentative LAB** are described as relying mainly on **glycolysis (Embden–Meyerhof–Parnas, EMP)** and producing **two molecules of lactate from one glucose**. (aguirregarcia2024lacticacidfermentation pages 2-4)
- **Heterofermentative LAB** are described as using the **pentose phosphate pathway with phosphoketolase** and producing **lactate, CO2, and either ethanol or acetate**. (aguirregarcia2024lacticacidfermentation pages 2-4)
- A 2023 mechanistic description explicitly notes heterolactic LAB convert glucose to roughly **equimolar lactic acid, ethanol and CO2 under anaerobic conditions**, with a net yield typically **~1 ATP per glucose**, and that additional ATP can be obtained when acetyl-phosphate is directed to acetate (via phosphate acetyltransferase and acetate kinase). (christofi2023mixedcultureanaerobicacidogenic pages 15-19)

### 1.2 Defining enzymes and metabolites
- **Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH)** is highlighted as the enzyme converting pyruvate to lactate in lactic acid fermentation contexts. (hakim2023acomprehensivereview pages 5-6)
- **Phosphoketolase** is identified as a key enzyme for heterofermentative metabolism in LAB. (aguirregarcia2024lacticacidfermentation pages 2-4, aguirregarcia2024lacticacidfermentation media cf6c2638)

### 1.3 Stoichiometric/energetic signatures (usable as assay expectations)
- Homolactic fermentation: one mole glucose → **two moles lactic acid + two ATP** (review-level theoretical statement). (hakim2023acomprehensivereview pages 5-6)
- Heterolactic fermentation: **~1 lactate/glucose** is theoretically expected (review-level) and product branching yields lactate plus ethanol/acetate and CO2. (hakim2023acomprehensivereview pages 5-6, aguirregarcia2024lacticacidfermentation pages 2-4)

## 2. Trait scope (TraitMech curation guidance)
### 2.1 What the trait represents
This trait should be curated as a **physiological capacity/metabolic mode** detectable by (i) end-product profiling (lactate accumulation), (ii) genomic presence of pathway-defining enzymes/modules (LDH for lactate formation; phosphoketolase route for heterofermentation), and/or (iii) growth/acidification phenotypes on sugars under oxygen-limited conditions.

### 2.2 Boundary cases and exclusions
- **Mixed-acid fermentations** where lactate is minor should not be labeled lactic acid fermentation without evidence of lactate-dominant flux.
- **Chain elongation** organisms that consume lactate (rather than produce it) should not be labeled as lactic-acid-fermenting, though lactate is a relevant community metabolite. (walters2023ametagenomelevelanalysis pages 9-11)
- **Host-context claims about D-/L-lactate** (animal metabolism/rumen impacts) are important for applied settings but may be outside the core microbial trait definition unless the graph explicitly models stereoisomer production as part of the phenotype. (zhao2024innovativelacticacid pages 3-5, aguirregarcia2024lacticacidfermentation pages 2-4)

## 3. Recent developments and latest research emphasis (2023–2024)
### 3.1 Mechanistic consolidation in recent reviews
Recent reviews (2023–2024) continue to emphasize the canonical split between **EMP-based homolactic fermentation** and **phosphoketolase-based heterolactic fermentation**, with explicit linkage to product profiles (lactate-only vs lactate + ethanol/acetate + CO2) and substrate scope (hexoses vs pentoses). (aguirregarcia2024lacticacidfermentation pages 2-4, hakim2023acomprehensivereview pages 5-6)

### 3.2 Community and systems-level perspective
Metagenome-level analysis in a long-running dairy coproduct bioreactor highlights that **feedstock composition** (e.g., lactose) selects for organisms encoding specific carbohydrate utilization pathways, and positions lactate as a **central intermediate** that can be produced by some taxa and consumed by others for downstream products (e.g., chain elongation to butyrate/hexanoate/octanoate). (walters2023ametagenomelevelanalysis pages 9-11)

### 3.3 Engineering and process determinants (beyond classical LAB)
A 2024 review of yeast/fungal lactic acid production describes oxygen/redox constraints in certain hosts (e.g., Crabtree-negative methylotrophic yeasts needing hypoxic conditions to ferment), and quantifies how oxygen level shifts can strongly increase byproduct formation (arabitol). These are relevant to *industrial implementations* of lactate production and to graph edges that relate oxygen to product branching (but are host-specific). (melo2024justaroundthe pages 9-11)

## 6. Current applications and real-world implementations (with recent quantitative data)
### 6.1 Food fermentation and biopreservation
A 2024 review emphasizes that LAB fermentation supports food preservation via acidification and antimicrobial metabolites (including bacteriocins), with time/temperature implementation metrics reported for specific bacteriocins in food matrices (e.g., pediocin inhibition periods in refrigerated foods; elimination of *Listeria monocytogenes* in milk within days). (aguirregarcia2024lacticacidfermentation pages 11-13)

### 6.2 Silage fermentation (agriculture)
A 2024 review on silage-related lactic acid production reports specific lactic acid concentrations in silage after inoculation and storage times (e.g., increases in g/kg lactic acid after 60 days) and provides an example of high-titer bioreactor lactic acid production and productivity in a 5 L system (114.6 g/L; 2.61 g/L/h), supporting the feasibility of strain/process optimization for lactic acid accumulation. (zhao2024innovativelacticacid pages 3-5)

### 6.3 Biorefinery and mixed-community fermentation
Metagenome-informed bioreactor work shows lactate is often an **intermediate** in community fermentations, feeding downstream products such as medium-chain fatty acids via chain elongation by Firmicutes, which is relevant for designing microbial consortia where “lactic acid fermentation” may be a first-stage function. (walters2023ametagenomelevelanalysis pages 9-11)

### 6.4 Industrial production in non-LAB hosts (engineering note)
Engineering-focused literature in yeasts/fungi highlights that oxygen availability can alter redox balance and byproduct formation: lowering O2 from 21% to 8% increased arabitol titers by ~220% in an engineered context, underscoring that oxygen is a key control variable when implementing lactate production in different hosts. (melo2024justaroundthe pages 9-11)

## 7. Expert synthesis (authoritative-source analysis)
Across recent reviews and systems studies, the most curation-stable mechanistic backbone for TraitMech is:
1) **Sugar catabolism to pyruvate**, 2) **LDH-mediated reduction to lactate** for redox balancing and ATP generation via substrate-level phosphorylation, and 3) branching to **heterolactic byproducts** (ethanol/acetate + CO2) when phosphoketolase-linked routes operate, with **oxygen and substrate type** as major determinants of product distribution and ATP yield. (aguirregarcia2024lacticacidfermentation pages 2-4, hakim2023acomprehensivereview pages 5-6, christofi2023mixedcultureanaerobicacidogenic pages 15-19)

The applied literature strongly supports modeling lactic acid fermentation as both (i) a **preservation/acidification function** in foods and silage and (ii) a **platform intermediate** in engineered consortia and biorefineries where lactate is subsequently upgraded. (aguirregarcia2024lacticacidfermentation pages 11-13, zhao2024innovativelacticacid pages 3-5, walters2023ametagenomelevelanalysis pages 9-11)

## 8. Key statistics/data points suitable for curation notes
- Homolactic theoretical stoichiometry: **1 glucose → 2 lactate + 2 ATP**. (hakim2023acomprehensivereview pages 5-6)
- Heterolactic anaerobic product profile: **roughly equimolar lactate/ethanol/CO2**, ~**1 ATP/glucose**, with potential additional ATP when acetyl-phosphate is routed to acetate (acetate kinase branch). (christofi2023mixedcultureanaerobicacidogenic pages 15-19)
- Industrial/bioreactor example (reported in review): **114.6 g/L L-lactic acid** in a 5 L bioreactor, **2.61 g/L/h** productivity. (zhao2024innovativelacticacid pages 3-5)
- Silage examples (reported in review): lactic acid increased to **82.12 g/kg after 60 days** with one inoculant; other inoculants yielded **47.8 g/kg** and **42.8 g/kg** vs **36.9 g/kg** control. (zhao2024innovativelacticacid pages 3-5)
- Oxygen shift in engineered yeast context: reducing O2 from 21% to 8% increased **arabitol titers by ~220%** (byproduct branching under redox stress). (melo2024justaroundthe pages 9-11)
