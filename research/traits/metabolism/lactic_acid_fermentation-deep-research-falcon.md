# TraitMech curation report: lactic acid fermentation

## 1. Trait record and scope

- **Trait label:** lactic acid fermentation
- **Trait identifier:** `traitmech:000026`
- **Category / kind / status:** METABOLISM / CLASS / REVIEWED
- **Parent:** `METPO:1002005`
- **Synonym:** lactate fermentation

### Operational definition

This trait is the microbial capacity to conserve energy by fermentatively converting carbohydrate-derived pyruvate mainly to lactate. ATP is generated principally by substrate-level phosphorylation in glycolysis, while lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) reoxidizes NADH to NAD+, allowing glycolysis to continue. In the canonical homolactic route, one glucose gives approximately two pyruvate, two lactate, and net two ATP; reported experimental lactate yields are approximately 0.74–0.99 g per g substrate versus a theoretical value near 1 g/g. (gayathri2023roleofaerobic pages 4-6, gayathri2023roleofaerobic pages 7-8, bintsis2018lacticacidbacteria pages 1-3)

The class should include both:

1. **Homolactic fermentation:** lactate is the dominant carbon end product, commonly through the Embden–Meyerhof–Parnas pathway.
2. **Heterolactic fermentation:** the phosphoketolase pathway yields lactate together with CO2 and ethanol and/or acetate. Product allocation depends on redox balance and growth conditions. (bintsis2018lacticacidbacteria pages 1-3, bintsis2018lacticacidbacteria pages 3-7)

### Boundary cases

Do **not** equate the trait with any observation of lactate production. The following should normally be excluded or separately represented:

- **Aerobic glycolysis/Warburg metabolism:** lactate production in oxygenated eukaryotic cells is not necessarily microbial fermentation.
- **Malolactic fermentation:** malate is decarboxylated to lactate and CO2; this is an acid-modulating conversion, not sugar-to-lactate energy metabolism.
- **Lactate oxidation or assimilation:** lactate is the substrate rather than the fermentation product.
- **Chemical lactic-acid synthesis** and polymerization to polylactic acid.
- **Mixed-acid fermentation with only minor lactate:** curate as lactic acid fermentation only if lactate is a defining or major end product under the assayed condition.
- **Genotype-only predictions:** the presence of an `ldh` homolog is insufficient because LDHs can differ in stereospecificity, direction, cofactor use, and physiological role.

Fermentation mode is also condition-dependent. Carbon limitation, temperature, pH, oxygen, and NADH-reoxidation capacity can redirect nominally homofermentative organisms toward mixed products. Obligately heterofermentative examples include *Lactobacillus brevis*, *L. fermentum*, and *L. reuteri*, whereas *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum*, *Lactococcus lactis*, *L. casei*, and *L. rhamnosus* can exhibit facultative heterofermentative behavior. (bintsis2018lacticacidbacteria pages 1-3, bintsis2018lacticacidbacteria pages 3-7)

## 5. Recent developments and real-world implementation

### Metabolic engineering and industrial production

A 2024 synthesis of yeast and filamentous-fungal engineering identifies LDH installation, competing-pathway deletion, hexose-transport enhancement, lactate-transporter engineering, redox balancing, and acid tolerance as major strategies. Reported examples included 102.3 g/L lactic acid in *Rhizopus oryzae* and 142 g/L in an engineered *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* strain. These are production-host results, not evidence that all such organisms naturally possess the target trait. (melo2024justaroundthe pages 5-6)

For glycerol-fed engineered yeast, a strain containing bovine L-LDH and deleted pyruvate decarboxylase achieved 0.67 g/g yield and 0.15 g·L⁻¹·h⁻¹ productivity. Lowering oxygen from 21% to 8% increased the competing coproduct arabitol by 220%, illustrating that oxygen and redox balancing can materially redirect carbon away from lactate. (melo2024justaroundthe pages 9-11)

Industrial neutralization commonly uses calcium carbonate because of its cost, but calcium lactate formation introduces a downstream sulfuric-acid purification requirement. The 2024 review therefore treats low-pH production and organic-acid tolerance as major process goals rather than ancillary phenotypes. (melo2024justaroundthe pages 11-13)

### Food fermentation

LAB starter cultures are implemented in dairy, vegetable, cereal, and beverage fermentations for rapid acidification, preservation, flavor development, and texture. However, the graph should distinguish the defining sugar-to-lactate module from secondary functions such as proteolysis, lipolysis, bacteriocin production, vitamin production, and exopolysaccharide synthesis. (bintsis2018lacticacidbacteria pages 1-3)

A 2023 cheese “digital twin” reconstructed 1,272–1,473 genes and 1,337–1,790 reactions per species for *L. lactis*, *L. plantarum*, and *Propionibacterium freudenreichii*. The model constrained oxygen transport to represent the microaerobic cheese environment and estimated species-specific growth rates of 0.187–2.17 mmol·gDW⁻¹·h⁻¹. This is a useful contemporary implementation of condition-aware community metabolism, but model-predicted edges should remain separate from experimentally confirmed edges. DOI: [10.1101/2023.05.05.539417](https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.05.05.539417), posted May 2023. (lecomte2023adigitaltwin pages 7-10)

### Silage and biomass valorization

Lactic fermentation is used in silage to acidify plant material, suppress spoilage, extend storage, and retain feed quality. Recent work emphasizes strain selection, substrate substitution, inoculation design, and process optimization. Lignocellulosic hydrolysates and agricultural residues are also being evaluated as lower-cost carbon sources. Mechanistically, these applications still require the same glycolysis–LDH–redox core, but hydrolysis, inhibitor tolerance, and mixed-sugar uptake should be represented as upstream modules rather than intrinsic parts of the trait.

## 6. Expert synthesis for YAML curation

### Recommended minimal conserved backbone

The existing 12-node/11-edge graph should, at minimum, preserve this chain:

1. glucose → glycolysis;
2. glycolysis → pyruvate;
3. glycolysis → ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation;
4. pyruvate + NADH → lactate + NAD+, catalyzed by stereospecific LDH;
5. regenerated NAD+ → continued glycolysis;
6. lactate/lactic-acid accumulation → extracellular acidification.

This backbone is both mechanistically defining and broadly portable. (gayathri2023roleofaerobic pages 4-6, melo2024justaroundthe pages 5-6, bintsis2018lacticacidbacteria pages 1-3)

### Recommended optional modules

- **Homolactic stoichiometry:** two lactate and net two ATP per glucose.
- **Heterolactic branch:** phosphoketolase → lactate + CO2 + ethanol/acetate.
- **Acid tolerance:** ATP → F1F0-ATPase-driven proton extrusion → pH homeostasis.
- **Product export:** organism-specific lactate transporter → extracellular lactate.
- **Environmental regulation:** oxygen/redox/pH/temperature/substrate annotations.
- **Application context:** fermented food, cheese, silage, or industrial bioreactor.

Experts increasingly treat product export, redox management, and acid tolerance as coupled determinants of production performance rather than independent engineering targets. Nevertheless, they should remain accessory causal modules because a strain can possess the biochemical fermentation trait without exceptional export or low-pH tolerance. (melo2024justaroundthe pages 11-13, melo2024justaroundthe pages 5-6)

## 7. Claims that should not yet be curated

1. **A universal lactate-export protein.** The retrieved Jen1 evidence is yeast-focused. LAB transporter identity must be validated by organism-specific genetics or transport assays.
2. **Universal anaerobic dependence.** Some LAB are aerotolerant, and oxygen can activate NADH oxidase, POX–ACK, respiration-like metabolism, or oxidative stress. The 2.3-fold anaerobic advantage is specific to the reported *L. plantarum* experiment. (gayathri2023roleofaerobic pages 7-8, zotta2017aerobicmetabolismin pages 11-14)
3. **Universal pH optimum of 5–6.** Treat this as assay-specific, not a class definition.
4. **A fixed heterolactic product equation containing both ethanol and acetate.** Electron acceptors and NADH-reoxidation capacity alter the branch ratio. (bintsis2018lacticacidbacteria pages 3-7)
5. **Universal D- or L-lactate stereochemistry.** Curate stereochemistry only when LDH identity or chiral product measurements support it.
6. **LAB taxon membership as proof of the trait.** LAB are physiologically heterogeneous, and expression can be substrate- and environment-dependent.
7. **`ldh` presence as sufficient genetic evidence.** Sequence annotation alone does not establish flux, physiological direction, or major-product status.
8. **Model predictions as experimentally validated causal edges.** Keep digital-twin and genome-scale-model predictions marked computational until experimentally tested. (lecomte2023adigitaltwin pages 7-10)
9. **Acid tolerance as required for trait presence.** It controls robustness and productivity but is not the defining catalytic mechanism.
10. **Recent engineered-yeast titers as natural microbial-trait evidence.** These support engineering principles and quantitative feasibility, not native phenotype assignment. (melo2024justaroundthe pages 5-6, melo2024justaroundthe pages 9-11)
